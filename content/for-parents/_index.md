## How to Help at Home

You do not need to speak fluent Kannada to support your child's learning. Here is what actually makes a difference:

**Ask your child to teach you.** After each Saturday, ask them to teach you one word or phrase they learned. This reverses the dynamic and makes them the expert — children retain language far better when they teach it.

**Play Kannada in the background.** InfoBells songs on YouTube during car journeys, a Kannada audio story at bedtime. Passive exposure adds up over weeks and months.

**Use the take-home card.** Each session we send home a card with that week's vocabulary words, a sentence starter to try, and specific suggestions for the week. Stick it on the fridge.

**Show genuine curiosity.** Ask about Karnataka — the food, the festivals, the stories. Children who feel their heritage is interesting and valued engage more deeply with the language.

## What to Expect

Community language learning works differently from regular school. Progress is slower and less linear — and that is fine. A child who attends consistently for two years and has even minimal home support will make meaningful progress. A child who attends inconsistently will struggle to retain anything.

Consistency matters more than intensity. One hour every Saturday plus ten minutes at home each week is far more effective than a two-hour session once a month.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**My child already speaks some Kannada at home — which level should they join?**
We will have a short informal conversation with your child before placing them. Parent self-reporting about language levels tends to be optimistic — a quick 10-minute chat with a teacher gives us a much more accurate picture.

**What if my child misses a session?**
Check the session notes page for their level — we post what was covered and the homework after every session. The vocabulary take-home card is also available to download.

**My child says they don't want to come anymore. What should I do?**
Talk to the teacher first. Sometimes it is a specific issue we can address. Sometimes it is normal resistance to something that requires effort. And sometimes the placement is wrong and moving levels makes a real difference.

**Can I sit in on a session?**
Absolutely for the first session. After that we find children engage better when parents are not in the room — they tend to look to parents for reassurance rather than attempting the language themselves.
