A Simple Guide for Parents: Our Kannada Learning Journey

Our goal is not perfect fluency overnight. It is to help children build confidence in Kannada, connect with family, and feel proud of where they come from.

What makes this program different?

Children learning Kannada abroad need a different approach from children growing up in Karnataka.

We teach through stories, songs, conversation, culture, and consistent routines, not just drills and worksheets.

Placement is skill-based: age matters, but confidence and readiness matter more.
Sessions are weekly: most levels meet on Saturdays for 45 to 75 minutes.
Progress is gradual: steady attendance and small amounts of home support make the biggest difference.

What children build over time

Level 1: listening, speaking, songs, and joyful first exposure
Level 2: alphabets and foundational vocabulary
Level 3: kagunitas, word formation, and short conversations
Level 4: ottaksharas, paragraphs, and confident expression
Level 5: presentations, essays, storytelling, and free expression

Level 1
Kannada Explorers

Ages 4-6

Play-based Kannada through rhymes, stories, action words, and theme vocabulary.

No reading or writing yet. The focus is comfort, rhythm, and participation.

Level 2
Akshara Learners

Ages 6-9

Introduction to swaras, vyanjanas, and simple words with everyday vocabulary.

Children begin reading, writing, listening, and giving 1-2 sentence responses.

Level 3
Word Builders

Ages 9-11

Kagunitas, word-building, simple sentence writing, and short conversations.

Students begin using Kannada more actively, not just recognizing it.

Level 4
Sentence Builders

Ages 11-13

Ottaksharas, paragraph writing, story retelling, and stronger reading confidence.

Students describe events and express ideas in connected sentences.

Level 5
Fluent Speakers

Ages 13-15

Reading short texts, writing reflections and essays, and speaking with confidence.

The goal is independent expression through presentations, conversation, and debate.

How parents can help at home

  • Ask your child to teach you one new word or phrase after class.
  • Play Kannada songs or stories in the background during car rides or bedtime.
  • Use the weekly take-home card for a short 5-10 minute practice moment.
  • Show curiosity about Karnataka, family stories, festivals, and food.

What to expect

  • Consistency matters more than intensity.
  • Children progress at different speeds, especially in a heritage-language setting.
  • It is normal for speaking confidence to grow before reading and writing do.
  • If placement feels off, we can adjust after observing your child in class.
Ten hours a term may not create fluent speakers, but it is enough to help children feel connected to family, culture, and language.
